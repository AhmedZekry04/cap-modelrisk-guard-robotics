ModelRisk-Guard: Runtime Verification and Self-Repair for LLM-Written Robot Procedures in Data-Centre Halls

AgentVerse 2026, Robotics track · Local LLM (Qwen2.5-Coder-7B via Ollama) + MuJoCo + browser dashboard · no cloud dependency

1. Who needs it and why

Unplanned data-centre downtime is estimated at $9,000 to $23,750 per minute (industry surveys), and one stalled 800G transceiver can stop a synchronised training job across thousands of GPUs. Halls are remote, hot and RF-shielded, so operators are trialling robots for hardware servicing. A manipulator following an ungrounded motion can snag the optical trunk cable next to the port and cause the outage it was sent to prevent. Code-as-Policies (Liang et al., ICRA 2023) gives zero-training flexibility, but a 7B model writes physically ungrounded motions: in our rack it pulls a transceiver 120 mm and sweeps into a fibre cable 45 mm behind the gripper. Static checks cannot see this.

2. What we built

  • Robot and sensors. A UR5e arm with a Robotiq 2F-85 gripper (MuJoCo Menagerie) on a mobile platform. Sensors are MuJoCo sensor elements, not simulator shortcuts: a wrist RGB-D camera (colour segmentation + aligned metric depth gives a “camera clearance” and a confidence state), a gripper touch sensor, joint encoders, and a 36-ray LiDAR on the platform. Geometry-derived numbers are labelled “simulator clearance” and shown separately.
  • Guard before motion. The model’s procedure is frozen (sha-256 policy id) and executed in four perturbed sandboxes (cable position ±5 mm, friction). Checks: peak cable force < 20 N (a configured simulation threshold), zero contact, ensemble variance (Radic Webster et al., 2021) and task completion (disengaged ≥ 30 mm, lifted, reseated).
  • Evidence-driven repair. The measured evidence (first contact 44 mm, peak 1.3 kN, admissible stroke window) goes back to the model, which rewrites its 13-line procedure (sensor pre-checks, grip, pull 0.120 → 0.037 m, 80 mm inspection lift, reseat, post-check). The revision is re-verified; only then does the exact approved text run through the restricted skill API. Every executed skill call is logged with the policy id and the page proves executed = approved. If nothing passes, the robot holds and says why.
  • Perception-driven correction mid-run. During the live pull the camera clearance is checked every 5 ms physics tick. An unscripted cable shift (button) drops it below 4 mm: the robot holds, the model re-plans from the measured travel, the re-plan is verified from that held state, then resumes. An occluded camera holds motion before it starts.
  • Navigation mission, same stack. “Go to Rack A and inspect it”: the model writes follow_route/inspect_rack; the guard runs a sandbox rollout plus LiDAR validation of every route. The maintenance cart is draggable live on the map; when the LiDAR stops the robot it re-plans from its current pose (R1 → R2), verified, up to four times.
  • Judge-facing dashboard. One main view, one code panel (original, revision diff, executed skills), one plain-English guard message, Original vs Guarded cards. Both paths start from an identical frozen policy and hashed simulator state; every number shown is derived from the run.

4. Measured results (local model, today)

Original 120 mm pull: contact at 48 mm, peak 1,269 N, FAILED in 4/4 sandboxes. Revision 37 mm: 0 N, minimum clearance 6.7 mm in the sandboxes and 8.0 mm live, camera 44.3 mm vs geometry 45.0 mm, COMPLETED with an 80 mm lift. A 20 mm pull is rejected by the task check alone (no contact, never disengaged). Occluded camera: HELD, no motion. Navigation with the cart dropped into the aisle mid-drive: LiDAR stop at (2.3, 0.0) with 0.44 m ahead, re-plan to R2 verified from that pose, Rack A reached and inspected after one re-plan. 26 automated tests across five suites pass.

5. Limitations and safety boundaries

The 20 N limit is configured, not measured from hardware; contact forces prove the collision, they are not calibrated. The arm follows the gripper by inverse kinematics (no joint dynamics); the cable is a rigid capsule on a spring carrier; camera depth is the renderer’s ideal depth; navigation pose is simulator ground truth and routes are fixed polylines (a re-plan picks another validated route). The system prompt carries a vendor 0.12 m stroke prior that makes the failure reproducible, and the 7B model needs the measured admissible window to repair reliably. Fail-safe by construction: no code moves the robot until it has passed the sandbox, and a hold is always a valid outcome.
